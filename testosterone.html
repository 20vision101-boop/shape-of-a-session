01 · THE PREMISE

Part One — One number, three problems

A total testosterone result is a single number with three things wrong with it. First, it's diurnal — it peaks in the morning and drifts down all day, so an afternoon draw can read low on a perfectly normal man. Second, it's variable: the same person tested on two mornings can differ enough to cross a reference-range boundary, which is why guidelines want two draws.

Third, and most importantly, roughly 98% of it is bound — held tightly by SHBG and loosely by albumin — and only the unbound fraction is active. Two men with identical total testosterone and very different SHBG have different amounts of usable hormone. That's the gap this page is mostly about.

What follows: the panel and what each marker is for, the levers that actually move it ranked by how good the evidence is, the workup a clinician runs, the honest ledger on replacement therapy, and a calculator for your own numbers.

Framing note: this page treats low testosterone as a medical question with a diagnostic pathway, not as an optimisation target. Symptoms plus two confirmed morning draws is the entry point, and a doctor is the person who interprets them.

02 · THE PANEL

Part Two — What each marker is for

Five values, each answering a different question. Reference bands below are typical adult male intervals — assays differ enough that your own lab's printed range is the one that counts. Enter your numbers in Part Six and they'll appear on these bands.

Total testosterone
Everything in the blood, bound and free. Morning draw, twice, before drawing conclusions.
Free testosterone
The unbound 1-2% that's actually active. Calculated from total, SHBG and albumin — or measured directly by equilibrium dialysis, which is the better test and the rarer one.
SHBG
The carrier. Raised by age, thyroid excess, liver disease and some drugs; lowered by obesity, insulin resistance and steroids. It's why total alone can mislead in both directions.
LH
The pituitary's instruction to the testes. With low testosterone, high LH points at the testes; low or normal LH points upstream — and upstream causes are the ones that are often fixable.
Estradiol
Made from testosterone by aromatase. Men need it for bone, libido and mood. Crushing it with an aromatase inhibitor causes its own problems.

04 · THE WORKUP

Part Four — What good care looks like

Not a protocol to run on yourself — a sketch of the standard sequence, so you can tell whether the care you're being offered resembles it. A clinic that skips straight from one afternoon blood draw to a prescription has skipped the part that finds the cause.

The branch in the middle is the whole point. Secondary hypogonadism — low testosterone with a low or normal LH — is where the reversible causes live: opioids, anabolic steroid use, obesity, sleep apnoea, energy deficit, thyroid disease, a prolactin-secreting tumour. Several of those are treatable, and treating them can put the axis back.

05 · THE LEDGER

Part Five — The honest ledger on TRT

Testosterone therapy works, in the narrow sense that taking a hormone raises that hormone. The question worth asking isn't whether it works — it's what it costs and what it forecloses.

What it does

  • Libido and sexual function — the most consistent benefit in trials of genuinely hypogonadal men.
  • Lean mass and strength — real, dose-dependent, and larger when combined with training.
  • Bone density — improves, which matters in long-standing deficiency.
  • Mood and energy — improves on average in deficient men; much less clear when levels start normal.

What it costs

  • Fertility — suppresses sperm production, sometimes permanently after prolonged use. Bank sperm or discuss hCG before starting, not after.
  • Your own production — shuts down while you're on it, and recovery after years is unreliable.
  • Haematocrit — rises, sometimes enough to need dose changes or blood donation.
  • Permanence — in practice it's usually a lifelong commitment with ongoing monitoring.
  • Sleep apnoea — can worsen untreated apnoea, which is why it gets screened first.

Questions worth asking before you start

  1. Have two morning draws confirmed it? One low afternoon result is not a diagnosis.
  2. Has anyone looked for a cause? LH/FSH, prolactin, iron studies, a weight and medication history, an apnoea screen.
  3. Do I want children, ever? This is the decision that's hardest to reverse.
  4. Who monitors me, and how often? If the answer is "we'll email you a refill", that's the answer to a different question.
  5. What happens if I stop? Ask before starting, not two years in.

The cardiovascular safety picture has improved with recent trial evidence in men treated for diagnosed hypogonadism. "Not shown to be harmful when treating a deficiency" is a much narrower claim than "safe for anyone who wants more", and the second one keeps getting made on the strength of the first.

06 · THE CALCULATOR

Part Six — Your panel, on your device

Total testosterone and SHBG are enough to calculate the free fraction using the Vermeulen equation — the same one most online calculators run. Values you enter are stored in localStorage, never sent anywhere, and also plotted on the reference bands back in Part Two.
