01 · THE PREMISE

Part One — You don't want less cortisol

Cortisol gets talked about as a toxin to be minimised. It isn't. It's the hormone that gets you out of bed: it mobilises glucose, sharpens attention, and holds blood pressure up. People who genuinely can't make enough of it are seriously ill.

What matters is the shape of the curve across the day. A healthy pattern spikes hard in the 30-45 minutes after waking, then declines all day to a floor around midnight. The pattern that tracks with chronic stress isn't a higher line — it's a flatter one: a weak morning rise and an evening that never comes down.

So the goal isn't suppression, it's amplitude. Everything on this page is aimed at making the morning steeper and the evening lower.

One caveat before the rest: persistent fatigue, unexplained weight change, dizziness on standing, or the pattern not responding to any of this are reasons to see a doctor rather than to try harder. Cushing's and adrenal insufficiency are real diagnoses with real tests; "adrenal fatigue" is not one of them.

02 · THE CURVE

Part Two — Three days, three shapes

Salivary cortisol across 24 hours, drawn three ways: the rhythm you want, the flattened slope that tracks with chronic stress, and the evening elevation that short sleep and late stimulants produce. Illustrative curves, not measurements.

Note what the three have in common: the total area under them isn't wildly different. It's the amplitude — the distance between the morning peak and the midnight floor — that separates them.

THE MECHANISM

The axis that makes it

The loop is self-limiting by design: cortisol shuts off the signal that produced it. Most of what's called "chronic stress" is that brake losing sensitivity under a load that never lets up.

04 · THE PRACTICES

Part Four — Fourteen things you can actually do

Each one carries a dose, an honest note on how good the evidence is, step-by-step instructions, and the caveats. Filter by what it works on, or by when in the day it belongs.

Ranked by leverage rather than novelty, the order is roughly: sleep regularity, morning light, caffeine timing, alcohol, then everything else. The breathing protocols are the fastest to feel and the least likely to change a weekly average on their own.

05 · THE DAY

Part Five — Where each one belongs

The same practices, arranged against the curve. Click a block to see what belongs in it and why.

The four that matter most

  1. Fix the wake time first. Everything else on this page is downstream of a stable clock, and the wake time is the anchor — not the bedtime.
  2. Light early, dim late. The two ends of the same lever. Ten minutes outside in the morning is worth more than an hour of anything else here.
  3. Move the caffeine, don't cut it. Timing beats abstinence for almost everyone. Delay the first, cut off the last.
  4. Watch the total load. Training stress and life stress come out of the same account. A hard block at work is a reason to deload, not to prove something.
